*This post is general information, not legal advice. Will requirements vary by state, so confirm the rules that apply to you with your state's statutes or a licensed attorney.*

## What is a will questionnaire?

In general terms, a will questionnaire is a worksheet of questions you answer before a will is drafted. It is a preparation step, not a legal instrument. A [last will and testament](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/last_will_and_testament) is the document that actually directs what happens to your property at death, and it has to meet your state's legal formalities to count.

The sources reviewed for this post did not include a government-published list of standard questionnaire questions, so this post does not claim one. What it can do is show you the legal tests your answers eventually feed into.

## Is a will questionnaire legally binding?

No. Nothing in the statutes reviewed here treats a questionnaire as a will. The legal requirements attach to the will itself, and they differ from state to state. The [Cornell Legal Information Institute's overview of a last will and testament](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/last_will_and_testament) is a good starting point, and your state's probate statute is the authority that controls. Confirm the signing and witnessing rules for your state directly on your state legislature's website or with a licensed attorney.

If you only fill out a questionnaire and never execute a valid will, you have not made a will. If you die without a valid one, your estate passes under [intestacy](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/intestacy) rules rather than under your questionnaire answers.

## Who is allowed to make a will?

In the four state statutes reviewed, the baseline is age 18 and sound mind:

- **Massachusetts:** an individual [18 or more years of age who is of sound mind may make a will](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-501).
- **Minnesota:** any person [18 or more years of age who is of sound mind may make a will](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/524.2-501).
- **Maine:** an individual of sound mind who is [18 or older, or a legally emancipated minor, may make a will](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec2-501.html).
- **Idaho:** any [emancipated minor or any person 18 or older who is of sound mind](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title15/t15ch2/sect15-2-501/) may make a will.

"Sound mind" is the legal idea of [testamentary capacity](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/testamentary_capacity). Questionnaire answers do not establish it. It is judged at the time the will is signed. Other states may differ, so check yours.

## What makes a will valid beyond the questionnaire?

Validity turns on your state's formalities, not on how thorough your notes were. This post does not state a specific signing, witness, notary, or handwritten-will rule, because those rules vary and were not verified against a government source for every state. Look up your state's probate code on your legislature's website, or ask a licensed attorney, before relying on any format.

After death, a will is usually submitted to a court in a process called [probate](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/probate), where its validity is examined. That is where defects in signing or witnessing tend to surface.

## What should I do after finishing a questionnaire?

Treat the questionnaire as step one. Next, turn your answers into a will, and confirm your state's execution rules before you sign. The capacity statutes linked above, such as those for [Massachusetts](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-501) and [Minnesota](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/524.2-501), show where to start looking in your own state's probate code. If your situation is complicated, a licensed attorney in your state can confirm the details.

## FAQ

**Is a will questionnaire legally binding?** No. A questionnaire collects information. A will has its own requirements, which vary by state; see [Cornell LII on wills](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/last_will_and_testament).

**How old do I have to be to make a will?** In Maine, Massachusetts, Idaho and Minnesota, 18 or older and of sound mind; see the [Massachusetts statute](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-501). Maine and Idaho also allow emancipated minors.

**Does a questionnaire prove I had capacity to make a will?** No. Capacity is judged when the will is signed; see [testamentary capacity](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/testamentary_capacity).

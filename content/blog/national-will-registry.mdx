*This is general information, not legal advice. Laws vary by state and change; confirm the rule for your state before relying on it.*

## Is there a national will registry in the U.S.?

We found no federal, government-run will registry. What the statutes we reviewed describe is state law: a handful of states let you deposit a will for safekeeping, and many states set rules for whoever holds a will after the maker dies. One federal regulation touches on will custody, but it is narrow: [25 CFR § 11.701](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-25/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-11/subpart-G/section-11.701) applies only in Court of Indian Offenses jurisdictions, requiring a custodian to deliver a will to that court within 30 days of learning the maker has died. It is not a national registry or a general federal rule for will custodians.

Private companies advertise "national" registries. We did not verify what those services do or cover against a primary source, so this post does not make claims about them.

## Can I file my will with a court or government office while I'm alive?

In some states, yes. Massachusetts lets a will be deposited with a court for safekeeping; the will is sealed and kept confidential ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)). South Dakota's statute also allows deposit with any court, sealed and confidential ([SDCL 29A-2-515](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/29A-2-515)). In Minnesota, the testator or an agent may deposit a will with any court for safekeeping ([Minn. Stat. ch. 524](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/524/pdf)).

North Dakota uses a different office: a will may be deposited with a county recorder, not a court ([N.D.C.C. § 30.1-11-01](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t30-1c11.pdf)). The state's courts confirm this in their self-help guide, noting that state law allows you to deposit your will with a North Dakota County Recorder ([North Dakota Courts, Wills Research Guide](https://www.ndcourts.gov/Media/Default/Legal%20Resources/Legal%20Self%20Help/Probate/Wills%20Research%20Guide.pdf)).

We did not confirm deposit rules or fees for other states, so check your own state's statute or court website.

## Who can get my will back while I'm alive?

Only you or someone you authorize in writing. In Massachusetts, during the testator's lifetime a deposited will may be delivered only to the testator or a person authorized in a writing ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)). North Dakota's statute is the same in substance: lifetime release goes only to the testator or a person authorized in a signed writing ([N.D.C.C. § 30.1-11-01](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t30-1c11.pdf)).

Both statutes require the deposited will to be sealed and kept confidential.

## What happens to a deposited will after I die?

The court acts once it learns of the death. Under Massachusetts law, the court must notify any person you designated to receive the will and deliver it on request, or it may deliver the will to the appropriate court ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)). Minnesota's version likewise allows the court, after being told of the testator's death, to deliver the will to the appropriate court ([Minn. Stat. ch. 524](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/524/pdf)).

## If someone is holding a will after a death, what must they do?

Many states impose a duty to deliver, and the timing varies by state. Massachusetts requires a person holding a will to deliver it within thirty days after notice of the death to someone able to secure its probate, or to an appropriate court if no such person is known ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-516](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-516)). Other states set similar 30-day delivery deadlines, including Rhode Island ([R.I. Gen. Laws § 33-7-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE33/33-7/33-7-5.htm)), New Hampshire ([RSA 552:2](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LVI/552/552-2.htm)), and Wisconsin ([Wis. Stat. ch. 856](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/856)).

Not every state uses a fixed number of days. Maine's statute requires delivery "with reasonable promptness" ([18-C M.R.S. § 2-515](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-c/title18-Csec2-515.html)). We did not confirm the deadline in other states, so check your own state's statute.

## What are the penalties for not turning in a will?

Some states attach liability. In Nebraska, a person who willfully fails to deliver a will is liable for resulting damages, and refusing after a court order is punishable as contempt ([Neb. Rev. Stat. § 30-2356](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=30-2356&print=true)). In Maine, a person who willfully fails to deliver, defaces, or destroys a will is liable for damages ([18-C M.R.S. § 2-515](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-c/title18-Csec2-515.html)). In Court of Indian Offenses jurisdictions, a custodian who fails to deliver a will within 30 days of learning of the maker's death is liable for damages ([25 CFR § 11.701](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-25/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-11/subpart-G/section-11.701)).

## What do the statutes say about where a will is kept?

The statutes reviewed describe two things. First, holders of a will in many states have a legal duty to deliver it after the maker's death, as in [M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-516](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-516). Second, a few states offer deposit with a court or county recorder, as in [M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515) and [N.D.C.C. § 30.1-11-01](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t30-1c11.pdf). We have only confirmed deposit for the states named above.

## FAQ

**Is there a national will registry run by the federal government?** We found no federal will registry. The deposit and delivery rules we reviewed are set by individual states ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)). Private registry services exist, but we did not verify their claims against a government source.

**Can I deposit my will with a court while I am alive?** In some states, yes. Massachusetts, South Dakota, and Minnesota allow deposit with a court, and North Dakota allows deposit with a county recorder ([N.D.C.C. § 30.1-11-01](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t30-1c11.pdf)). In Massachusetts, South Dakota, and North Dakota the will is sealed and confidential, and in Massachusetts and North Dakota it is released during your lifetime only to you or a person you authorize in writing.

**What happens to a deposited will after I die?** In Massachusetts, the court notifies the person you designated and delivers the will on request, or delivers it to the appropriate court ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)). Minnesota's statute likewise lets the court deliver it to the appropriate court after being told of the death ([Minn. Stat. ch. 524](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/524/pdf)).

**How long does someone holding my will have to turn it in?** It depends on the state. Massachusetts requires delivery within thirty days after notice of the death ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-516](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/Section2-516)), and Rhode Island, New Hampshire, and Wisconsin set similar 30-day delivery deadlines. Deadlines vary by state, and Maine requires delivery with reasonable promptness ([18-C M.R.S. § 2-515](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-c/title18-Csec2-515.html)). Willful failure to deliver can mean damages in Maine and Nebraska, and contempt in Nebraska after a court order ([Neb. Rev. Stat. § 30-2356](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=30-2356&print=true)).

**Does depositing a will make it valid?** We did not find a primary source saying that depositing or registering a will changes its validity, so we make no claim either way. The statutes we reviewed address safekeeping and delivery, not execution requirements ([M.G.L. c. 190B § 2-515](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-515)).
